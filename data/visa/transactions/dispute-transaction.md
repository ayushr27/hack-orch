# Disputing a Visa Transaction

You may dispute a transaction if it was unauthorized, if you didn't receive goods or services, or if you were charged the wrong amount.

## When to Dispute

- **Unauthorized charge**: You didn't make the transaction and don't recognize it.
- **Duplicate charge**: The same transaction appears twice.
- **Wrong amount**: The merchant charged more than the agreed price.
- **Goods/services not received**: You paid but didn't receive what you ordered.
- **Credit not processed**: A merchant agreed to a refund but it never posted.

## How to File a Dispute

1. Contact your card issuer first (number on back of card).
2. Explain the disputed charge — have the date, amount, and merchant name ready.
3. Your issuer will initiate a chargeback with Visa on your behalf.
4. You may be asked to submit a written dispute and supporting documents.

### Typical timeline
- Initial review: 1–3 business days.
- Provisional credit (your account credited temporarily): within 5 business days.
- Final resolution: up to 45–90 days depending on merchant response.

## What You Need to Provide

- The transaction date and amount.
- Merchant name.
- Evidence: order confirmations, correspondence with merchant, photos if goods were damaged.

## Dispute vs. Contacting the Merchant

For billing errors and returns, try contacting the merchant first — it's usually faster. Only escalate to a dispute if the merchant refuses to resolve the issue.

## Fraud vs. Dispute

If you suspect fraud (you made no such purchase and don't recognize the merchant), call your issuer immediately to report fraud — a dispute is filed automatically and your card may be reissued.
